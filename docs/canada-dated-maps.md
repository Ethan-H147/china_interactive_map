Canada’s historical snapshots show the year-end division of present-day Canadian land from 1866 through the current build date. The 1866 reconstruction starts from the Atlas of Canada’s 1867 geometry, merges Canada East and Canada West into the Province of Canada, and restores the colonial status of Nova Scotia and New Brunswick. The independent colonies, Rupert’s Land, and the North-Western Territory keep their own cards. This view is about colonial and federal political divisions; it does not map Indigenous territories or treaty rights.

[The Atlas of Canada’s territorial evolution series](https://open.canada.ca/data/dataset/e88ce995-b69a-4595-a752-bb06b061b5a3) supplies the documented changes, including districts of the Northwest Territories and the Ontario–Manitoba dispute. [Canadian Heritage’s boundary history](https://www.canada.ca/en/canadian-heritage/services/historical-boundaries-canada.html) provides chronology. Some early lines are generalized. The historical international frontier is limited to the present-day Canadian footprint, so the map cannot reproduce the historical extent of competing cross-border claims. The 1903 Alaska boundary decision is annotated.

Both views split the present-day Canadian land polygons into dated ownership or electoral areas. Overhangs are clipped away; uncovered shore pieces are assigned by shared shoreline contact and local adjacency. The historical snapshot is one dated mosaic in the same format as the United States history (`dist/historic-borders.mjs`, docs/us-history.md): each division keeps a version while its shape and description are unchanged, all versions are cut into shared faces in `history/borders.bin`, and a year only assigns faces to divisions, so changing year or selecting a division needs no further geometry. The United States snapshot draws Rupert’s Land from the same file. Election ridings still use one file per representation order. The map workers keep a compact copy of the two most recently used riding files, so selecting a riding builds only its outline. Ridings are coloured by feature state, so moving between elections that share a representation order only repaints them. Present-day international borders, water and modern subdivision assets remain independent of the timeline.

Federal election results cover the 45 general elections from 1867 to 2025. Sources are the [Library of Parliament](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/ea8f2c37-90b6-4fee-857e-984d3060184e) and [Elections Canada](https://www.elections.ca/content.aspx?dir=rep/off/45gedata&document=summary&lang=e&section=res) for 2011–2025. Dated geometry is adapted from the [CC0 riding archive by Taylor, Lucas, Kirby and Hewitt](https://doi.org/10.5683/SP3/4E8DCR) and [Elections Canada’s 2023 representation order](https://www.elections.ca/content.aspx?dir=cir/mapsCorner/edb&document=index&lang=e&section=res). Three Ontario ridings omitted in the 1905 export use their 1903 polygons; the 1905 revision primarily reflects new western provinces. Early reconstructed riding lines are approximate. Unrepresented areas are gray; a district that elected members of different parties has a neutral color and lists both winners. Acclamations do not contribute invented votes.

To refresh the local assets, run `scripts/fetch-canada-dated-maps.ps1` from the atlas root (the source archive is about 456 MB), then `npm run build:canada-dated-maps` and `npm run test:canada-dated-maps`. The browser loads one riding file at a time and caches only recent revisions.
