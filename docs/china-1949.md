# Republic of China historical snapshot

Local route: `#china-1949`. Open **Historical snapshot** using the button beside the China header. ROC is hidden from the country list; the snapshot remains a child view of China. **Present day** returns to the modern atlas.

The reference date is **1949**, with province polygons from the **1947–1949** configuration of Christian Henriot's ENP-China dataset, [Provinces in Republican China (boundaries)](https://www.virtualshanghai.net/Data/Tables?ID=198), ERC grant 788476. The downloaded shapefile archive is retained under `scripts/boundary-sources/china-1949/`, with its URL, CC0 license, retrieval date and SHA-256 checksum in `manifest.json`. `node scripts/build-china-1949.mjs` rebuilds the public dataset without downloading new data.

The map shows 35 provinces, including Chahar, Jehol, Xikang, Suiyuan and the nine northeastern provinces, plus Tibet Area, the Outer Mongolia reference area and Hainan Special Administrative Region. Hainan island components are separated from Guangdong's source geometry for its April 1949 status; offshore jurisdiction is not reconstructed. Local names use traditional Chinese; historical English aliases such as Rehe, Peking, Hankow and Tientsin remain searchable.

The [user's administrative reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_(1912%E2%80%931949)#Administrative_divisions) supplies the twelve special-municipality identities. They are selectable city-location points, at the same administrative rank as the provinces, and are also available in the subdivision dropdown. No current municipal boundary or invented buffer is substituted for a historical boundary. The province dataset does not cut those municipal territories out; this limitation is visible in the snapshot's coverage note and city cards. Points denote city locations, not surveyed historical centroids.

This is a nominal administrative reference, not a military-control map. Tibet's de facto autonomy, the distinction between mapped and controlled territory in Xikang, Soviet administration of Dalian, and Communist administration of Harbin are noted. Outer Mongolia is included for historical context using the Mongolia feature from the same CC0 dataset’s earlier 1912–1921 configuration. This outline includes Tannu Uriankhai (Tuva); it is not represented as ROC-controlled territory in 1949. The visible selection footnote links the [January 1946 communiqué recognizing Mongolian independence](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1946v10/d958). The [National Archive of Tuva](https://gosarhivrt.ru/?p=13369) documents its incorporation into the USSR in 1944. The additional source archive and checksum are retained in `outer-mongolia-manifest.json`; the historical area card identifies the earlier geometry date. The source's approximate coasts, lake treatment and international limits are retained. Historical mapped areas are labelled as calculated historical geometry; contemporary population and GDP are not shown as historical statistics.

Capital dots cover all 35 provincial capitals and the three regional seats. Their reference names, approximate city coordinates, aliases and citations are retained in `scripts/boundary-sources/china-1949/capitals.json` and copied to the public dataset. Historical seats include Zhenjiang (Jiangsu), Guilin (Guangxi), Wuchang (Hubei), Dihua (Xinjiang), Guisui (Suiyuan) and Liaoyuan at present-day Shuangliao (Liaobei). They represent the reference administrative scheme, not every wartime relocation. Clicking a capital selects its province and shows the reference capital; the layer has its own saved-view toggle.

The snapshot uses the shared regional sidebar, flag position, search, language controls, selection, saved views and mobile sheet. Its dataset loads only on request. The scene hides the replaced modern China/Mongolia preview fills and excludes those countries from both coarse fallback land layers, including late arrivals. Those layers return when leaving the snapshot. It also temporarily hides modern boundary linework and restores each layer's requested visibility on return, including layers that arrived or changed while the snapshot was open. The modern world-land builder explicitly excludes snapshots so a later rebuild cannot turn historical territory into a present-day country mask.

The global country-menu browser checks cover keyboard navigation and verify that snapshots are not listed as countries.
