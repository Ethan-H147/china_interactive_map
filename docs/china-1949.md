# Republic of China historical snapshot

Open **Historical snapshot** on the China map and choose **1949**. The year shares China's historical timeline, map, selection and sidebar. The Qing and 1949 steps retain the camera. Old `#china-1949` links open `#china/history=1949`; old shared-view links also restore the historical selection and camera.

The snapshot shows the nominal administrative divisions, using [Christian Henriot's 1947–1949 reconstruction](https://www.virtualshanghai.net/Data/Tables?ID=198), ENP-China, ERC grant 788476, CC0. The reconstruction supplies the provincial lines, with their original level of detail. The atlas supplies the physical land geometry. The historical reconstruction supplies province lines and substantial extensions beyond the present-day border. The build keeps source-backed pieces at least 75 km² in area with land at least 2.5 km from the edge of the extension. Smaller and narrower seam mismatches follow the atlas geometry. Uncovered modern land uses the nearest historical division. Internal lines and selection outlines are drawn from joined provincial shapes. This is an administrative reference, not a map of military control.

There are 35 provinces, Tibet Area, Hainan Special Administrative Region, and 12 selectable special-municipality location dots. Historical municipal boundaries are unavailable. The provinces and areas retain their historical names and references, and the capital toggle shows the 38 reference provincial capitals and regional seats. Hong Kong and Macau use their modern land outlines and are shown as other land. The Beihai–Qinzhou and Fangcheng coast is shown with Guangdong, with Shangsi in Guangxi, based on the [Guangdong administrative history](https://www.gdhmo.gov.cn/gaikuang/content/post_68514.html) and the [Hepu gazetteer](https://www.bhlib.cn/d/file/2023-11-10/3352ac45bc6d3b75b425f3045953d5a8.pdf). Western Ili follows the Khorgos frontier documented by the [1881 Treaty of Saint Petersburg](https://www.istmat.org/node/27245) and the [U.S. boundary study](https://library.law.fsu.edu/Digital-Collections/LimitsinSeas/pdf/ibs064.pdf).

Jiangxinpo is shown as an approximate historical claim in northern Burma. Selecting it opens its own card and highlights the claim. Its outline was traced from [Historical Begonia Leaf Map of China](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Historical_Begonia_Leaf_Map_of_China.svg) by Chatsven, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), smoothed at the reference scale and clipped to atlas land in Burma. The [1960 boundary treaty](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%201010/volume-1010-I-14847-English.pdf) documents the later settlement.

The [ROC Administrative and Claims map](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ROC_Administrative_and_Claims.svg) by ZanderSchubert, after Electionworld and Pryaltonian, [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/), supplies a later schematic reference for externally administered claims. The map uses atlas outlines for Mongolia, Tuva, Arunachal Pradesh, the eastern Pamirs and the Diaoyutai/Senkaku islands. The Wakhan, Karakoram, Bhutan and Amur sectors are approximate, clipped to the atlas’s corresponding administered land. These claim areas share Tibet Area’s fill. The ROC recognized Mongolian independence in January 1946, and Tuva was already part of the Soviet Union in 1949.

The source archive and attribution are under `scripts/boundary-sources/china-1949/`. `node scripts/build-china-1949.mjs` rebuilds the retained 1947–1949 geometry and point inputs. `npm run build:china-history` fits them to the atlas land and writes the existing shared China history pack. `npm run test:china-history` checks the common geometry and timeline, and `npm run test:china-1949` checks source integrity and the 1949 cards, points and links.
