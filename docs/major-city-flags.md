# Major Canadian and Brazilian city flags

Reviewed 31 October 2026. Scope: the 50 largest municipalities in each country plus every national, provincial, state, and territorial capital. Rankings use the retained 2025 population snapshots: [Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1710015501) and [IBGE](https://apisidra.ibge.gov.br/values/t/6579/n6/all/v/9324/p/2025?formato=json).

Canada now covers all 56 cities in scope. 53 already had flags; this change adds Terrebonne, Saanich, and Iqaluit. Existing Canadian flags and population ranking remain unchanged. Brazil has 53 city flag associations: 52 new artwork assets plus the existing Federal District flag associated with Brasília.

Only adopted/current municipal flags are included. Belo Horizonte retains its traditional flag after the rejected 2024 proposal. Belford Roxo uses the adopted 2025 design. Brasília is the Federal District rather than a municipality, and shows its official District flag once. Saanich uses the district flag supplied by its administrative assistant, not the police flag or a neighboring municipality. Iqaluit uses its current design, not the 2013 proposal.

The [source manifest](../dist/data/major-city-flag-sources.json) retains city identities, evidence, artwork attribution, licensing, processing methods, dimensions, and content hashes. Third-party artwork is a reproduction of an official flag; its image license is recorded separately from the flag’s official status. Terrebonne is extracted from the city’s own vector diagram on page 45 of its graphics manual, excluding diagram guides and frame. Other assets are unmodified source images or Wikimedia service renderings with lossless display thumbnails. Clicking flags opens the retained larger artwork and credit.
