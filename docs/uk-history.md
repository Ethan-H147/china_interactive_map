# UK historical snapshot

Open **Historical snapshot** from the United Kingdom map. The timeline starts with the Acts of Union in 1707 and shows every year-end through 2025. Use the arrows or the Left, Right, Home and End keys to move through the timeline. The map remembers the last year. **Present day** returns to the regular UK map.

Click a territory or use the sidebar search and **Nation or territory** picker to find possessions outside the British Isles. The legend distinguishes the home nations, Crown dependencies, colonies, chartered companies, protectorates, dominions, mandates, joint administrations and disputed claims. Dates and sources appear in Map settings.

## Coverage

Britain and Ireland follow the Acts of Union and the creation of the Irish Free State. The historical view colors the same first-level land polygons as the present-day UK map, grouped by nation. It uses the atlas’s present UK–Ireland land frontier from 1922 onward. The overseas layer uses Cliopatria for changing extents in regions including early North America, India, southern Africa and Burma. The atlas's Canadian and U.S. historical data supply several detailed North American boundaries. Georgia’s southern charter and the Floridas share the retained Newberry US mosaic, with West Florida’s northern extension dated to 1764 ([USGS Bulletin 1212](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/1212/report.pdf)). Selected overseas territories reuse the modern atlas's coordinates for the years when their mapped extents match. Hong Kong uses the same coastline at every stage, with the island shown first, then southern Kowloon and Stonecutters Island, then the New Territories. Australia and the other regional colony layers derive both overview and close views from the modern atlas’s detailed physical coastline. Historical divisions determine ownership inside that land. Its dated colonial divisions include the inland limits that predate the present states. The Fiji outline gains Rotuma in 1881. Aldabra, Desroches and Farquhar move from Seychelles to the British Indian Ocean Territory in 1965 and return to Seychelles at independence in 1976. Reused coastlines reflect the modern source. Several dated territory records still use approximate outlines where year-specific geometry is unavailable. The catalogue covers the major overseas territories and many smaller possessions. It includes claims and indirect administrations where identified and leaves out many local changes, brief military occupations and Indigenous boundaries. See [sources and coverage](../dist/data/europe/united-kingdom/history/sources.json).

The builder packs the historical faces, the shared modern land and the edges into one download. A configuration assigns each face its historical unit and stores the edges to draw. The 319 timeline entries reference those configurations. Adjacent years with the same configuration reuse the geometry, colors and lines in the browser. At zoom 7, the timeline loads one local coastline file where additional detail is available. Those files retain the same dated face IDs. Every regional overview is simplified together from its detailed modern-land ownership mesh at a 0.001° tolerance (about 110 m, below a pixel at these overview zooms). It is a display level of the same source, not an independent historical shoreline. Regional overview borders use the same interior paths as the detail, with physical coastline strokes excluded at both scales. Selection outlines always read the canonical regional files, including below the detail threshold, and retain the same source URL through zoom changes. The North American build cleans collapsed overlay fragments before clipping to land and checks that clipping cannot increase coverage. Administrative strokes require land on both sides of each segment; physical coasts and collapsed seams keep their fill geometry without a border stroke. Physical water cutouts cover the older shore where it extends past the detailed land. The shared modern overview stays accurate below this threshold, so the high-detail source no longer needs to cover a broad regional view. The regional files list the atlas sources they use. The modern coast is a geographic reference, so the detail includes later shoreline changes such as reclamation.

Historical fill sources retain exact paths at every zoom. Simplifying neighboring chronology cells independently can open diagonal seams or collapse narrow pieces; the shared snapshot renderer now keeps those paths aligned. Only cells used by the current configuration, including the gray land fallback, expand into the tile source. The original packed chronology remains available; years with the same configuration reuse their source. Home-nation highlights use the modern map’s existing land source. Regional coastline inputs use the same buffered-view clipping as the detailed modern background, so distant coastline vertices do not accumulate in the tile index. Every original vertex and water hole inside that buffered view remains intact. Selected outlines omit legacy survey edges surrounded by the same selected territory. Verified physical coast paths and water-hole rings keep their coordinates and the existing adaptive line profile.

Clicking another country's gray background land keeps the historical snapshot open. Return to **Present day** to resume switching countries by clicking the map. The country selector remains available.

## Local checks

- `npm run build:uk-history` applies the retained physical US coast, then rebuilds the packed map from the retained source subset and other atlas files.
- `npm run test:shared-physical-coast` checks lagoon water, modern subdivision coverage, all regional overview inputs, multiple-region selections, and rendered pixels across the zoom handoff.
- `npm run test:uk-history` checks the dated geometry, then exercises the timeline in Chrome on desktop and mobile.
- `node scripts/browser/run.mjs historical-fill-integrity history-navigation modern-context` checks solid selected fills, real versus internal borders, detail handoffs, and map-click navigation.
- `node scripts/validate-outline-detail.mjs` checks unchanged outline coordinates, true borders, overlapping selected pieces, and narrow water holes.

The build inputs include a cached subset of [Cliopatria v0.2.1](https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria/tree/v0.2.1), by Bennett et al., under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), and the relevant [Natural Earth v5.1.2 map units](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/v5.1.2/geojson/ne_10m_admin_0_map_units.geojson), in the public domain. To regenerate the subset, pass `--import path/to/cliopatria.geojson path/to/ne_10m_admin_0_map_units.geojson` to the builder. The atlas clips and simplifies the imported geometry and combines it with the dates and geographic references in `scripts/uk-history-territories.mjs`.

The southeastern US modern map uses a retained OpenStreetMap physical coast to remove marine water included in the administrative source. The same corrected first-level land feeds British ownership, modern context and the gray fallback. The retained input, query, bounds, checksum and ODbL attribution are in `scripts/boundary-sources/united-states/physical-coast/southeast.json`. Directed coastlines are polygonized once during the build, then compiled at the existing regional atlas detail budget of about 33 m. The full source is retained. Existing Census ownership, subdivision IDs and reported statistics remain intact; this does not reconstruct eighteenth-century shore erosion or reclamation.

Anticosti reuses the complete modern Quebec island polygon from 1763 while the dated Canadian unit controls its status and end date. The historical renderer also clears excess gray background land in the sea using a derived coast-water asset. That asset compares both global gray meshes with modern Canadian coastal land, protects international land frontiers, uses the existing North American sea mask, and tiles only a buffered viewport in the worker. It does not assign land ownership or change inland political borders.

The inland St. Croix border lake cluster uses solid display geometry in background, selected-country, quiz, riding and historical views. Its country split follows the retained IBC survey and its subdivisions use the existing dividers. The historical mesh retains the original dated owner assignments; only the local physical fill and lake-edge arcs change. Unrelated lakes, coastal bays, identifiers, results and reported statistics are retained. Rebuild with `npm run build:maine-border-fill`.
