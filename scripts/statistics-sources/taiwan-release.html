GDP: Preliminary Estimate for 2026Q2 and Outlook for 2026-27

Contact Information : Economic Forecast Section, Department of Statistics, Directorate General of Budget, Accounting & Statistics (DGBAS)
Tel : 886-2-2380-3487

August 14, 2026　 For the second quarter of 2026, Taiwan’s real gross domestic product (GDP) grew by 12.93% from the same period of previous year according to the latest preliminary estimate, and the growth rate of 2026Q1 has been revised to 15.43%. Furthermore, the real GDP is predicted to grow 11.05% and 6.04% in 2026 and 2027, respectively. In 2025, the income quintile share ratio per household was 6.17 and the Gini coefficient was 0.341.

Preliminary Estimate of Real GDP in 2026Q2

   The real GDP grew by 5.70% on a quarter-on-quarter, seasonally-adjusted annualized basis (saar) and expanded by 12.93% on a year-on-year basis (yoy, all growth rates hereafter are on the same basis) in 2026Q2, 0.01 percentage point higher than the advance estimate.

   Meanwhile, the economic growth rate of 2026Q1 has been revised to 15.43% (formerly 14.55%).

    On the demand side, real exports of goods and services grew by 21.24% in 2026Q2, mainly driven by the sustained strong external demand for artificial intelligence (AI) and related evolving applications. Imports also grew by 18.25%.

    Regarding gross capital formation, investments in machinery and equipment, intellectual property products and construction increased. However, investment in transportation equipment decreased. Combining inventory changes, real gross capital formation expanded by 16.20%.

    In addition, real private final consumption grew by 5.91%, mainly driven by expenditures such as information and communication, entertainment, transport and flourishing outbound tourism, as well as the securities and funds transaction fees boosted by the booming stock market.

    On the production side, the manufacturing sector grew by 18.27% in 2026Q2, following the 26.17% increase in the previous quarter, mainly due to the output expansion of semiconductors, computers, electronics and optical products.

    The wholesale and retail trade sector increased by 18.45%, after the expansion of 14.71% in the previous quarter.

    The transportation and storage sector grew by 3.82% in 2026Q2, due to the growing demand for air freight transportation.

    The financial and insurance sector increased by 28.42% in 2026Q2, following the 15.19% growth in the previous quarter.

Forecast for 2026

    As AI applications expand from cloud model-training to inference, agentic AI, and edge devices, triggering greater demand for computing power, major cloud service providers (CSPs) are ramping up AI infrastructure development. Demand for both general-purpose AI accelerators and customized chips is rising simultaneously, fueling sustained and robust growth in hardware upgrades and deployment volumes. Consequently, exports from Taiwan’s highly competitive AI supply chain have surged significantly. Meanwhile, exports from traditional industries have likewise picked up. Moreover, prices of key components, such as memory, have risen due to tight supply-demand conditions. In aggregate with services exports, real exports of goods and services will grow by 21.28% in 2026.

   Real private consumption is projected to grow by 4.76%, supported by robust corporate earnings driving the stock market to record highs, as well as continued improvement in the labor market, which together have boosted household wealth and income. In addition, the automobile market has recovered as tariff concerns fade, and the booming stock market has fueled a surge in securities and fund transaction fees, both contributing to the momentum of consumption growth.

   Real private fixed capital formation is anticipated to increase by 11.58%. To meet robust global demand for AI infrastructure-related hardware, with buyers securing capacity via advance payments and long-term agreements, domestic supply chains, including semiconductors, packaging and testing, memory, IC substrates, and equipment, have been actively expanding capacity, fueling strong momentum in plant and equipment investments.

   Combining the above components as well as the public sector, Taiwan’s real GDP is projected to grow by 11.05% in 2026, revised upward by 1.41 percentage points from the previous forecast.

   Regarding the Consumer Price Index (CPI), since the outbreak of the conflict in the Middle East in late February, the government's price stabilization measures have effectively mitigated the impact on domestic prices. However, due to last year's low base effect, recent weather condition, and rising costs for consumer electronics components fueled by AI demand, commodity price growth has gradually expanded. Meanwhile, with a strong domestic economy and rising wages, the upward momentum in service prices is expected to continue. Overall, the CPI will increase by 2.07%, revised upward by 0.14 percentage point.

Outlook for 2027

    Although major CSPs face pressure from shrinking free cash flow, their capital expenditures are expected to continue expanding, boosted by the long-term growth trend of AI. As domestic manufacturers accelerate the expansion of advanced capacity to meet robust customer demand, alongside steady growth in R&D, the momentum in export and investment growth is anticipated to persist. Combining the contribution from other domestic sectors, real GDP will grow by 6.04% in 2027. The consumer price inflation will moderate to 1.90% as global commodity prices stabilize.

Income Distribution in 2025

    In 2025, the average disposable income per household was NT$ 1,210 thousand, representing the 3.9% increase from 2024. The median disposable income per household was NT$ 1,018 thousand, reflecting the 3.4% growth. After adjusting for household size, the average disposable income per person was NT$450 thousand, representing the 7.4% increase compared to the previous year, while the median disposable income per person was NT$367 thousand, reflecting the 3.2% growth.

   The income quintile share ratio per household was 6.17, an increase of 0.03 from the previous year. The income quintile share ratio per person was 3.73, down by 0.18 from the previous year.
